## Assignment Overview

Research art, design, and technical projects that have happened within the last 12 months. Find examples of things you thought were impossible that people are now making. Emphasize work that is open-source, well documented, forkable, remixable, or otherwise shareable.

Your post should describe what each project does, why it surprised you, and what design question it opens for your own work in this course.

## Requirements

- Write a blog post of approximately 500 words
- Include 2-3 illustrated examples from contemporary art, design, or technical practice
- Prioritize open-source repositories, public process notes, documentation, or other shareable work
- Include links to each project or source you discuss
- Caption every image and use descriptive alt text when you add images to Canvas
- End with one design question that could shape your own experimental phone interaction

## Research Focus

Look for examples that connect to one or more of these course themes:

- Agents, automation, and delegation
- Human judgment inside AI coding workflows
- Experimental interactions for phones and small screens
- Open-source or forkable creative technology projects
- Public documentation that makes a project easier to understand, test, or remix

## Submission Details

Submit the post through Canvas by class on Sep 29, 2026.

Bring one example to discuss in class. Be ready to explain what the project does, what surprised you, and what question it raises for design practice.

## Rubric

### Research Quality (30%)

4 -- Examples are current, relevant, and well sourced
3 -- Examples are relevant with adequate sourcing
2 -- Examples are loosely connected or missing important source context
1 -- Examples are unclear, outdated, or unsupported

### Analysis and Surprise (30%)

4 -- Clearly explains why each example seemed newly possible or surprising
3 -- Explains most examples with some thoughtful analysis
2 -- Descriptions stay mostly at the surface
1 -- Little analysis beyond naming the examples

### Connection to Course Questions (25%)

4 -- Strongly connects examples to agents, phone interactions, openness, or AI-assisted design practice
3 -- Makes a clear course connection
2 -- Course connection is present but thin
1 -- Course connection is missing or unclear

### Presentation and Documentation (15%)

4 -- Clear writing, useful links, image captions, and accessible image descriptions
3 -- Complete and readable with minor issues
2 -- Missing some links, captions, or accessible descriptions
1 -- Difficult to follow or missing key submission pieces

## Instructor Note

This assignment is not about proving that a tool is magical. It is about looking carefully at what people are actually making, how they share the work, and what questions their examples open for your own design process.
